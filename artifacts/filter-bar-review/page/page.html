Filter bar — design review

Design review · filter & sort region

The toolbar is the only part of the app with no design system

Five directions, each designed to a distinct thesis and then independently critiqued against the app's hard constraints. Every specimen below renders under the same masthead and switch at a true 375px, so the only variable is the region itself.

What's actually wrong

Measured in a real 375px viewport against 333 line items, 150 orders and 379 sellers drawn from your own OrderWand export.

Vertical cost

171px at rest, 253px with chips open, 285px with custom dates — 21–35% of the screen before one package.

Shape language

Two radii (999px and 8px), two font families, three type sizes — all at identical height, background and border.

Native controls

The <select> draws its own caret; the range renders two grey mm/dd/yyyy boxes in the system font.

Double selection

"30d" is filled accent violet twice — once on the collapsed pill, again in the chip row below it.

No grid

Seven rows, four alignments: left, right, left, left, full, left, right.

Unstable across views

Tally's longer sort labels break search and sort onto separate rows, so the region changes height when you switch view.

Notices collide

272 lines outside range butts against 31 canceled — view with no separator; one underlined, one not.

No state summary

272 of 333 lines hidden in that screenshot, and nothing says so without reading every control.

The specimens

Scores are from an independent critic that saw each direction's markup and CSS but not the others. Nothing scored above 6/10 — read that as "no direction is ready to build as drawn", not as a tie.

Reviewer's verdict

This is the best-reasoned direction of its kind — the shape-carries-meaning argument is real, it genuinely dissolves problems 5, 6, 7 and 8 (measured: the placeholder needs 135.2px in a 164.1px field, so it fits whole), it uses no colour literals, no media queries beyond `prefers-reduced-motion`, no `<select>` and no `type="date"`, and its contrast pairs check out (inkSoft/paper 4.68, card/accent 5.19, red/redSoft 4.76). But its headline is wrong: measured side by side at 375px it saves 6px at rest, not 21 — the 171px baseline it quotes includes the view switch's own 14px margin, which the direction does not remove — and it is 4–13px TALLER in every open state plus a 127px push on sort where today's native select pushes nothing. Against that thin gain it trades away a working date picker for two fields whose separator cannot be typed on an iPhone keypad, drops the free "N days" input entirely, and ships a `:active` rule that blanks every accent-filled token while your thumb is on it. The idea deserves another pass; this draft is not the one to build.

What it gives up

Measured in a real 375px viewport, not estimated. Resting: 150px vs today's 171 — a 21px win, smaller than it sounds, because two of the four rows (the 34px search field and the 34px file-actions row) are irreducible and I kept both. The real win is that those 150px now *say something*. Open, it is a wash and in one case a slight loss: range chooser with a preset ≈ 241px (today 253), the sort chooser ≈ 278px (today 253), and the custom range ≈ 283px (today 285). I did not buy a smaller open state; I bought a smaller resting state and a legible one. Second: the custom range is typed, not picked — two mono `YYYY-MM-DD` fields on rules, numeric keypad, no calendar. That is the honest cost of killing the grey UA date inputs and their ~140px minimum. Anyone who wants to browse to a date has lost that; the seven presets have to carry it. Third: interaction cost. Sort goes from one native-select interaction to two taps (open the token, pick the word). The date presets were already two (disclosure + chip), so no change there, and Hide received stays one tap. A returning power user who knows exactly where the "45d" chip lives will find prose slower to aim at than a fixed grid — this direction optimises for "what is filtering my list?" over "hit 45d fast". Fourth: the layout is content-dependent in a way a chip row is not. Wrapping depends on label length, so a long pair ("dearest per copy" + a custom date token) can push the Find clause to a third line, +41px. It never overflows, it just gets one line taller — but the region no longer has a fixed height, and anything downstream that assumed one is wrong. Fifth: it is prose, so it is English-only and has to be *maintained* as prose. Two vocabularies (Packages/Tally) already, and every new filter has to find a grammatical home rather than just another chip slot. There is no room for a fourth clause without a third line. Sixth: two accent-filled tokens can be lit at once (narrowed range + hiding received), which is more accent on screen than today's single active chip. That is truthful — two filters are on — but it is louder.

7 constraint findings
  • THE HEADLINE COMPARISON IS FRAME-MISMATCHED. I built the proposal's exact markup/CSS and a faithful replica of today's region (from src/app.jsx: `ctl`, `chip`, `ctlSelect`, `dateInput`, `marginBottom: 10` on the date block and `16` on the toolbar) and measured both in a real 375px Chrome viewport with Cochin present. Proposed resting region = 150.5px — their number is correct. Today's SAME REGION = 156.5px (date row 54.5 + 10 + toolbar 76 + 16). The quoted 171px baseline additionally contains `.mdl-switch { ... margin-bottom: 14px }` (src/app.jsx:3045), which this direction does not touch: 156.5 + 14 = 170.5 ≈ the 171 they cite. Like-for-like the saving is 6px, not 21px — and problem 1 (vertical cost) is half the stated rationale for the whole direction.
  • EVERY OPEN STATE IS TALLER THAN TODAY, not "a wash". Measured at 375px, same frame: range chooser open on a preset 243px vs today's 239px (+4); range open with the custom fields 283.5px vs today's 270.5px (+13); sort chooser open 277.8px, i.e. a 127px push, where today's `<select>` pushes 0px because its popover overlays. The direction's own open-state numbers (241/278/283.5) are right; they are compared against the inflated 253/285 baseline, which is what turns a loss into a claimed wash.
  • THE FREE "LAST N DAYS" OPTION HAS NO INPUT. `<button class="opt" type="button" role="option">the last <span class="num">21</span> days</button>` — the number is a static span inside a button. Nothing in the markup or CSS lets a user set N; `.sub` is the only revealed slot and it is styled exclusively for two `.dt` fields (`flex: 1 0 100%` + two `width: 108px` inputs). One of the seven required date options is not reachable as drawn.
  • THE CUSTOM RANGE CANNOT BE TYPED ON THE TARGET DEVICE. `<input class="dt" inputmode="numeric" placeholder="YYYY-MM-DD">` — iOS's `inputmode="numeric"` keypad is digits only; there is no `-` key (nor on `tel`). The placeholder demands a character the keyboard does not offer, and no auto-formatting is specified. This replaces a control that was ugly but worked (`<input type="date">`, wheel picker, no typing) with one that is unusable on the only platform this app ships to. It also introduces an iOS focus-zoom where none existed: a `type="date"` field opens a picker without zooming; `.dt { font-size: 12.5px }` will zoom.
  • A LIT TOKEN GOES BLANK WHILE PRESSED. `.dir-sentence .tok:active { background: var(--paper); }` has specificity (0,3,0); `.dir-sentence .tok--on { background: var(--accent); color: var(--card); }` has (0,2,0), so `:active` wins regardless of source order. Pressing an accent-filled token (a narrowed range, or "hiding received") paints `--card` text on `--paper`: 1.05:1, invisible for the duration of the touch. Every accent-filled control in this region has this bug, and `-webkit-tap-highlight-color: transparent` removes the only other press feedback.
  • THE CUSTOM-RANGE ROW HAS EXACTLY ZERO HORIZONTAL SLACK AT 375px. Measured: `.sub` right edge = 348.0px, `.chooser` content edge = 348.0px (359 − 1px border − 10px padding). Nothing spare. At 320px it overflows its own panel by 24px, because `.dt { width: 108px }` inputs do not shrink (form controls resolve `min-width: auto` to an intrinsic minimum), which contradicts the claim of "0 at 320" — the region there measures 184.3px, not the claimed 170px.
  • TOUCH TARGETS BELOW THE STATED 34px FLOOR. Measured: `.dt` = 30.5px tall (`padding: 7px 2px 8px` + 12.5px mono + a 1px rule), and `.note-link` = 32px including its `::before { inset: -8px -5px }` pad. `.find-in` lands at 33.8px. Only `.tok`/`.opt` (35.8px with their pads) and `.act` (34px) clear CTL_H.
10 fixes that would raise it
  • Restate the vertical claim honestly — 150.5px vs 156.5px like-for-like, a 6px win — and re-argue the direction on the state-summary benefit alone, which is the half that actually holds. If the pitch needs a real height win, it has to come from the two rows kept untouched (the notes line and the 34px file-actions row), not from merging the filters.
  • Give the free-N option a real input. Reuse the `.sub` slot with a single mono field on a rule (`the last [ 21 ] days`), and specify that picking the option focuses it — otherwise cut that option and let the presets carry it, but say so.
  • Fix the date entry for iOS. Either three separate numeric fields (Y / M / D) with no separator to type, or auto-insert the hyphens on input and switch the hint to `inputmode="numeric"` + a documented mask. Pin `.dt` to 16px to stop the focus zoom and to 34px to meet CTL_H, and give the pair `flex-wrap: wrap` so it cannot overrun the chooser below 375px.
  • Add `.dir-sentence .tok--on:active { background: var(--accent); }` (or move the press feedback to `opacity`/`box-shadow`, which is state-independent). Same audit for `.opt:active` if a lit option ever becomes a button.
  • Buy back slack in the chooser: shorten `from`/`to` to a single `–` between the fields, or let `.sub` wrap. Zero slack at the design width means the first label change, locale, or font-metric difference on a real iPhone spills.
  • Specify the date token's format for a multi-year ledger — this app is deliberately the system of record for orders older than 120 days, so a cross-year custom range is normal. Measured, "12 Apr 2025 – 3 Aug 2026" re-wraps `.say` from 68.5px to 98.9px (+30.4px). Either fix the token to a mono fixed-width form that cannot re-wrap the sentence, or accept that picking a range moves the chooser under the thumb mid-interaction.
  • Make the chooser visibly belong to the token that opened it — a caret notch, or render it inline under the clause rather than as a full-width panel that looks identical for dates and for sort. Two different vocabularies appearing in the same box in the same place is the orientation cost of the one-slot design.
  • Give the search rule more signal than `--line`, which measures 1.45:1 on `--card` and 1.3:1 on `--paper`. A 1px rule at that contrast plus the word "Find" is thin evidence that a text field exists; consider a `--inkSoft` rule or a faint `--card` fill behind it.
  • Reconcile the radius. The region standardises on 3px while sitting between a 999px pill (`.mdl-switch`) and 10px package cards, and the file it lives in uses 8px fourteen times. "One radius" is true only inside this region; either extend the 3px vocabulary outward in the same commit or match what is already there.
  • Spell out both sort vocabularies. Only one label ("most still owed") is given; five Packages labels and six Tally labels have to be written, and the direction's own stated risk — that prose must be maintained as prose — is unmanaged until they are.

Reviewer's verdict (on the first pass)

The measurements are scrupulously honest — I rendered it and got 159px resting / 264px open / 0 overflow against claimed 159.5 / 264.5 / 0, zero colour literals, one media query, no select, no date input. It also scores a real invariant win: the fixed-fraction grid genuinely fixes a live reflow bug, since app.jsx:3262 really does swap "Hide received" for "Showing remaining only" inside a flex-wrap row today. But it fails the brief's headline problem and then breaks two things. What it gives up

Measured, not estimated — rendered at a real 375×812 viewport with the palette and font stack above: resting 159.5px (rows 59.5 / 39 / 37 plus two gold hairlines and 10+12px margins), open 264.5px, horizontal overflow 0. 1. The open state is WORSE than today in the common case: 264.5px vs 253px for a preset, though better than the 285px custom case. I restyled the file-actions row, which you said not to redesign. Conceptually it is untouched — Re-import, a Sync disclosure carrying its last-push date, Reset with the inline two-tap (shown armed in the open fragment as a redSoft wash reading "Tap again") — but leaving three beige lozenges under a ruled form would have destroyed the thesis. 8 constraint findings — addressed above
  • TOUCH TARGET, measured: the custom date fields are 18.5px tall, not 34px. `.writeon { padding: 1px 2px 2px; font-size: 12.5px }` inside `<div class="cell cell--write">` — and it is a DIV, not a <label>, so the surrounding 34px is dead space. Measured hitHeights.writeonInput = 18.5. The self-check claim "write-on row 34px" measures the row, not the tappable input. This is the smallest touch target in the app by a factor of two.
  • FUNCTIONAL BREAK ON THE TARGET DEVICE: `<input class="writeon" inputmode="numeric" value="2026-05-01">`. iOS renders inputmode=numeric as the 10-key pad — digits only, no hyphen. The user physically cannot type a date into the custom range on the one device this app runs on. inputmode="tel" doesn't help (+ * # only). This is not the disclosed "users will type more" tradeoff; it is "users cannot type at all".
  • REACHABILITY: only SIX date options ship. `RANGES` in src/app.jsx:1090 has SEVEN — `["all",...],[30],[45],[60],[90],["days","# days"],["custom","Custom…"]`. The panel renders All time / 30 / 45 / 60 / 90 / Custom. The `days` preset has no selector anywhere in either fragment, and the prose escape ("LAST [21] DAYS under any preset") is incoherent — an editable 21 sitting under a selected "30 days" chip does not say which range is active. The free N-days entry is asserted, not designed.
  • AS DELIVERED, NONE OF THE CSS APPLIES. Every rule is scoped `.dir-ruled .row`, `.dir-ruled .cell`, and the root element in both HTML fragments is `<section class="ledger-head">`. There is no `.dir-ruled` anywhere in the markup. I had to add the class by hand to measure anything.
  • "Sizing is clamp() throughout" is false. Four fixed sizes: `.caret { font-size: 8px }`, `.dot { font-size: 11px }`, `.writeon { font-size: 12.5px }`, `.act .when { font-size: 9px }`. Measured computed values confirm 8 / 11 / 12.5 / 9px, and the 8px caret is the sole tap affordance on the date control.
  • GOLD SCOPE: CLAUDE.md states --gold "exists for the masthead rules and nothing else". `.dir-ruled .rule { background: var(--gold) }` adds two more, and the "masthead's rules continued" framing is broken by the violet view-switch pill sitting between them. Four gold rules now share the screen; the bottom one lands 12px above the first package card.
  • iOS FOCUS ZOOM: `.find` computes to 14.25px and `.writeon` to 12.5px, both under Safari's 16px threshold, and build.mjs:19 is `width=device-width, initial-scale=1, viewport-fit=cover` — no maximum-scale. The app already sets `fontSize: 16` on its other free-text inputs (app.jsx:1491, 1663) for exactly this reason. Focusing the date blanks scales the whole page.
  • `.act { white-space: nowrap }` carries no overflow:hidden/text-overflow, unlike every other text run in the region. If the Sync date string ever renders longer than "9 Aug" it escapes its cell rather than ellipsising.

Reviewer's verdict (on the first pass)

The measurements are unusually honest — I rebuilt the markup and CSS verbatim and got 133px resting, 531px drawer (claimed 530), 664px open, notes strip stable at 29px even with worst-case text. But the 133px is only reachable because the file-actions row silently hangs 7.5px off the left edge of the screen with the app's real `Sync · pushed MM-DD` label; fix that properly and the resting cost is 175px, which is worse than the 171px it set out to beat. So the headline claim does not survive contact. The genuine achievement is elsewhere and the direction undersells it: this is the only shape I can see that kills the 253px and 285px states outright, and it is airtight on invariant 5 — in-flow drawer, no reveals inside it, presets change fills only, nothing reflows the list while a thumb is over it. Add a distinct active treatment (accent dot, or accent text) that is not the same token as 'panel is open'.
  • Decide what the count does during check-in. '23 of 136' is presented as the summary of what you are looking at, but under Hide received the sticky rule deliberately keeps completed rows on screen — so the number will disagree with the visible list on every tap. Freeze it with the sort snapshot, or the status line's central promise is false precisely while the user is working.
  • Reconsider dropping 'N lines outside range'. The ratio conflates three causes (date filter, search, hide received) into one figure, so the user can no longer tell which control is hiding data — a loss against problem 6, not a gain. Consider keeping the count as the ratio but attributing it on the date trigger itself.
  • Widen .clr to 34px and raise the search field's clamp floor from 15px to 16px — at 375px the clamp resolves to 15.01px and iOS will zoom on focus, which is a poor showing for the control the whole direction is named after.

Where this lands

  1. The Docket Drawer — the only shape that deletes the 253/285px open states outright, is airtight on invariant 5, and carries zero unverifiable platform risk; both its flaws are arithmetic, not architectural.
  2. The Ruled Foot — right instinct (controls belong where the thumb is, reporting belongs where the eye is), best-organised control surface in the batch, but gated behind an untested iOS visualViewport keyboard shim and a brief violation it has no good answer to.
  3. The Query Line — the only honest, independently verified resting cut that leaves the file actions alone (144px), but it ships a conditional-sort rule that is the exact bug class CLAUDE.md records as group 28, plus a 32px overflow in the steady state.
  4. The Filter Is A Sentence — the best writing and the only direction that dissolves problems 5, 6, 7 and 8 at once, but the headline is 6px like-for-like and it is taller in every single open state.
  5. The Ruled Head — the prettiest and the least tappable: it builds controls out of the same parts as the masthead's read-only tallies, so nothing looks touchable, and an 18.5px date input is half the app's own floor.

Recommendation — The Docket Drawer

It is the only direction whose failures are arithmetic rather than structural: the 175px regression is caused entirely by a dropped `flex-wrap` plus the real `Sync · pushed MM-DD` label, and both are one-line fixes that put it back at ~133px, whereas Sentence's and Ruled's ~6–13px gains are the ceiling of what those shapes can ever deliver. It is the only shape that kills the variable 253px/285px open states outright instead of trading them around, which matters more than the resting number — those are the states you actually hit when you touch a filter. It scored 5/5 on invariant safety for a real reason: the drawer is in flow, nothing reveals inside it, presets change fills only, and nothing can reflow the list while a thumb is over it — no fixed positioning to fight the sticky running head at z-index 30, and no keyboard shim that only a real iPhone can validate. Critically, it is the one direction that reframes the date crux correctly: you do not need to replace `<input type="date">`, you need to stop looking at it, and a drawer nobody opens during normal use makes the UA chrome a non-problem for free — where Sentence, Ruled and Progressive all replaced a working picker with fields that cannot be typed on an iPhone. Its two genuine weaknesses (search goes one tap deeper and you type blind; the word "date" appears nowhere at rest) are exactly what the runner-up grafts fix.

What every direction missed

The most valuable output of the exercise. These are failures common to all five, so they'd have survived any pick.

  • THE BASELINE IS WRONG FOR EVERYONE, AND PROBABLY OPTIMISTIC. All five argued against 171px, but ~14px of that is the view switch's own `margin-bottom: 14px` (src/app.jsx:3045), which no direction touches — the honest region baseline is 156.5px, so every headline saving in the batch is 14px overstated. Worse, the documented 258px / 171px figures in CLAUDE.md were clearly measured with the bare `Sync` label; the app actually renders `Sync · pushed MM-DD` after the first push, and the file-actions group carries `flexWrap: 'wrap'` — so on the author's own phone that row has probably already wrapped to two lines and the region is TALLER than 171 today. Nobody measured the baseline in the state the author's device is actually in.
  • NOBODY ACTUALLY TRIED TYPING A DATE ON AN IPHONE. Three of five (Sentence, Ruled, Progressive) independently shipped `inputmode="numeric"` with a `YYYY-MM-DD` placeholder. iOS's numeric keypad has no hyphen key. Three separate designers made the identical unforced error, which means none of them held a phone.
  • THE BEST ANSWER TO THE DATE CRUX WAS NEVER PROPOSED: don't type dates at all, pick them from the data. The ledger already knows every order date. A custom range could be month chips derived from the actual orders present ('Aug · Jul · Jun · May…'), which needs no keyboard, no native control, no masking, no validation, no clamping of 2026-02-31, is mono and 34px by construction, and reads in the register's own voice. For a ledger that is deliberately the system of record for orders older than 120 days, month-scoped browsing is probably what the author actually wants — and it makes the ~140px UA minimum width that started this whole problem irrelevant.
  • EVERY DIRECTION REARRANGED; NONE SUBTRACTED. Seven presets for one axis (All / 30 / 45 / 60 / 90 / free-N / custom) is the root cause of nearly every measured failure in the batch — the seventh chip, the wrapping, the untypeable fields, the 140px minimum. Nobody asked whether 45d and 60d earn their place, or whether free-N and custom are the same control. Drop two presets and fold free-N into custom and the layout problem substantially dissolves before any redesign is needed.
  • NOBODY DESIGNED THE ORPHANED VIEW OR THE EMPTY LEDGER. The region is hidden entirely under Orphaned, but the file actions must remain reachable there, and the whole region is gated on `… || !!window.remote` precisely because an empty ledger is when Pull matters most (CLAUDE.md group 28). All five designed the Packages/Tally case only. That is exactly where the last shipped bug lived, and the method lesson CLAUDE.md draws from it — 'the suite was all green and the feature was broken in its most important scenario, because every test booted a ledger that already had data' — applies verbatim to this batch of proposals.
  • NOBODY ACCOUNTED FOR THE STICKY RUNNING HEAD. Only Ruled Foot mentioned the stacking order, and it got it wrong (z-index 30, not 20, at src/app.jsx:3025). Sentence, Ruled, Drawer and Progressive all open in-flow panels without stating what happens when you open one while scrolled — the panel's top rows can render beneath the pinned head.
  • NOBODY PROPOSED A SINGLE ASSERTION. This is a codebase whose test suite is mutation-tested and whose author documents that 'an assertion that can't fail is decoration'. The batch is entirely CSS and measurements. And the deeper problem nobody named: jsdom has no layout, so NONE of these height claims can ever be protected in CI. A 171→133 win is unenforceable and will silently rot the way the pre-CTL_H toolbar did. If the resting height matters, something has to hold it — a documented measurement ritual at minimum, or a Playwright check.
  • TOUCH TARGETS WERE SELF-REPORTED WRONG IN FOUR OF FIVE. Every direction claimed 34px compliance and every reviewer found sub-34px controls by measuring the tap target rather than the well (18.5px, 28.5px, 30px, 32px). The pattern is that designers measured containers; the app's constant is about thumbs.

Grafts worth taking

Where the real answer usually lives — the winner's shape carrying the runners-up's better ideas.

  • Take the prose voice of the resting line from The Filter Is A Sentence, because the Drawer's terse mono docket strip scores 2/5 on discoverability and Sentence's reviewer confirmed the prose form dissolves problems 5, 6, 7 and 8 at once — graft the voice and the state-summary-as-resting-state idea, NOT the tap-the-word-to-open-a-panel mechanic, which is what causes Sentence's 127px sort push where the native select pushed 0.
  • Take deep-linked clauses from The Filter Is A Sentence: make each clause of the docket line open the ONE drawer scrolled to that section (tapping the range clause opens it at dates, the order clause at sort). This keeps the Drawer's single-surface simplicity while fixing its worst finding — that the word 'date' appears nowhere at rest, so a first-time reach for 'last 45 days' has nothing to aim at.
  • Take the always-visible search field from The Ruled Foot and The Query Line, and keep it OUT of the drawer. The Drawer's own reviewer named this its highest-value regression: the manila preview reports date-range counts, not search results, so 'did my Black Lotus arrive?' goes dark exactly while you type it. Search is a live control used against a visible list; filters are a rare deliberate act. Do not bury them at the same depth.
  • Take `max-height: min(60vh, 460px); overflow-y: auto` on the panel from The Ruled Foot's fix list, and measure against the TALLY sort set, not the Packages set. Six longer labels wrap to three rows; the Drawer measures 572px in Tally and its DONE button lands ~93px below the fold on a standard iPhone. Both directions sized their panel against the smaller vocabulary.
  • Take the fixed-fraction grid discipline from The Ruled Head for any state-dependent label. Its reflow finding is real in shipped code — `hideDone ? 'Showing remaining only' : 'Hide received'` sits inside a `flexWrap` row at src/app.jsx:3262, so that toggle resizes its own row today. Inside the drawer this is harmless, which is a further argument for the drawer; but the rule to keep is that any label that changes with state gets a fixed slot.
  • Take 'do not delete a control conditionally' from The Query Line's critique as a hard rule for the drawer's contents. Progressive's dropping of the sort scope when the result set is small is the same bug class as the group-28 Pull-unreachable-on-empty-ledger bug CLAUDE.md documents twice. Every control in the drawer must be present unconditionally.
  • Take the shape vocabulary from The Filter Is A Sentence but resolve it outward, not inward: give the drawer's mutually-exclusive selectors 999px (they are the same species as the `.mdl-switch` pill directly above, which is 999px at src/app.jsx:3042) and reserve the 8px rect for actions. Both Drawer and Sentence solved 'two radii inside the region' by inventing a third radius that disagrees with the switch — that moves the seam rather than closing it.

The crux

Two problems every direction had to solve: replacing the native select and the native date pair, and cutting the resting height.

NATIVE `<select>` — genuinely solved by two. The Docket Drawer and The Ruled Foot both replaced it with chips inside a surface that is already a deliberate two-tap destination, so the replacement costs nothing: there is no popover to emulate because you are already inside the panel. The Filter Is A Sentence and The Ruled Head replaced it with an in-flow chooser that pushes 127px of content down, where the native select's popover pushes 0 — that is a measured regression traded for a cosmetic win, and neither direction disclosed it. The Query Line replaced it and then specified that the sort scope disappears when the result set is under a screenful, which makes a required function unreachable exactly when you have filtered down to the set you want to reorder; that is worse than a UA caret and has to be withdrawn, not tuned. NATIVE `<input type="date">` — nobody solved it, and three directions actively broke it. Sentence, Ruled Head and Query Line all shipped `inputmode="numeric"` fields with a `YYYY-MM-DD` placeholder; iOS's numeric keypad has no hyphen key, so the custom range is literally uncompletable on the only device this app ships to. That is not a replacement, it is a removal disguised as a redesign, and Ruled Head compounds it by dropping the free "# days" preset entirely — six of the seven options in RANGES (src/app.jsx:1090) ship. Ruled Foot claims both natives are gone and was not caught on it, but its date entry is unverified and it never states what the input actually is. The Docket Drawer is the only direction that faces the crux honestly, and it does so by reframing it: keep `type="date"` for the first cut, because the drawer has already made the ugliness invisible. That is the correct answer. The problem was never that the native date picker is bad — it works, it validates, it needs no masking and cannot produce 2026-02-31 — the problem is that it is ugly in a place you look at all day. Move it somewhere you look at twice a year and the crux evaporates without inventing a picker. 171px — three of five dodged and one cheated. Verified like-for-like: Sentence saves 6px, not 21. Ruled Head saves ~12px at rest and is 11px WORSE open, with 14px of what it does save spent restating "187 LEFT / 136 PKGS" that the masthead already shows 100px above. The Docket Drawer measures 175px once you restore the `flex-wrap` it silently dropped — worse than the status quo, though fixable in one line. Only The Query Line cut it honestly and independently (144px verified) while leaving the file actions alone, and only The Ruled Foot cut it structurally (48px in flow) — but its real permanent chrome is 132px, not the claimed 98px, and it gets there by relocating Re-import / Sync / Reset to the foot of a 136-package list, which the brief explicitly forbade and which regresses the "recovery controls must not be gated behind the state they recover" lesson CLAUDE.md records as learned twice. The honest conclusion is that the 171px framing itself is the wrong target: 14px of it is the view switch's margin nobody touches, and the resting cost was never the real injury. The injury is the 253px and 285px open states, and exactly two directions delete those outright — the Drawer and the Ruled Foot.

Smallest first step

Do not build the drawer. Make one throwaway commit against the current region that changes nothing structural, and live with it for one mail day. (1) Add a single prose docket line ABOVE today's existing controls, stating the live filter state in the register's voice and absorbing the notices that already wrap to their own line — roughly fifteen lines of JSX, no controls moved, no behaviour changed, fully reversible. (2) In the same commit, temporarily set the Sync chip label to bare `Sync` and measure the file-actions row at 375px. That single day answers the two questions the entire batch turns on, and the answers are worth more than any of the five proposals. From (1) you learn whether a prose state-summary actually reads at 375px with your real values — a cross-year custom range like "12 Apr 2025 – 3 Aug 2026" is normal in a ledger that keeps orders older than 120 days, and if that wraps the line to two rows then Sentence, Ruled, Drawer, Ruled Foot and Query Line ALL have a problem, because every one of them rests on a summary line. You also learn the thing that decides the Drawer outright: once you can read the state, do you ever look at the chips again? If you stop, folding them away is proven by use rather than argued by pixels. From (2) you learn whether the Drawer's 133px is real or whether it is 175px — that one label is the entire 42px difference between beating the status quo and losing to it, and it is also the current region's undocumented overflow. Only after both come back green should you build the drawer, and build it in this order: file actions row fixed first, then the drawer, then search stays outside it, then the date field last — keeping native `type="date"` for the first cut, because by then it lives somewhere you never look.
