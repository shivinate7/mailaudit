4 · The Letter

The app writes you a short letter about today's mail. You answer by touching its words.

Central idea

The app writes today's mail as a short letter, and you answer by touching its words instead of working a list.

A mail-day session

  1. Open the phone flat. The letter reads: what is out, what came, what is late.
  2. Open an envelope. Touch the sentence that names its seller.
  3. Touch a phrase like "three of four Lightning Bolt". The reply bar counts copies.
  4. A finished sentence strikes through. The letter rewrites its totals.
  5. A blank envelope: tell it one card name. The letter names the best fit.
  6. A late seller gets a paragraph with Stamp it lost and Mark refunded.

Better than a phone list

It says what matters today in plain words, with nothing to filter. Late mail and closing claims arrive as sentences you cannot miss.

It reads like the stationery the palette already suggests.

Biggest risk

Prose does not scale. A 40-card order becomes a wall of words. Phrases are small tap targets, so one-handed use leans hard on the reply bar.
