All five concepts

1 · Card in Hand

The card you hold is the question. Type a few letters, and the open phone tells you which envelope it came from and what else should be in it.

Central idea

The card in your hand is the search; the app answers with its envelope and its envelope-mates.

A mail-day session

  1. Open the phone flat. The left page waits for a name.
  2. Pick up a card and type its first letters.
  3. The right page shows the envelope it came from and the cards that should travel with it.
  4. Tick its envelope-mates on the right page. One typed name covers the whole envelope.
  5. Tap + per copy in hand. Bought 4, got 3 reads 3/4. Next card clears the query.
  6. A name two envelopes share is held as a stray. The next card from that envelope decides it.
  7. A late envelope waits in Elsewhere, with Lost and Refunded beside it.

Better than a phone list

You never scroll to find the order. The card finds it. Keys and answer show at the same time, so the thumb never leaves the keys.

Most no-name envelopes stop being a special case. Their cards name the sender.

Biggest risk

Each envelope costs a few keystrokes before any tick. A name in many open orders, like a common reprint, needs a second card to decide.
