3 · Sorting Frame

Find an envelope by what you can feel. Its thickness picks the row, its age picks the column, and each pigeonhole holds only a few envelopes.

Central idea

Match an envelope by what your fingers already know, its thickness and its age, before you read a single card.

A mail-day session

  1. Open the phone flat. Every expected envelope sits in a pigeonhole: thickness down, days out across.
  2. Pick up an envelope and feel it. Tap Slim. Only that row stays lit.
  3. A named envelope: tap its pigeonhole, then tick its cards. Kestrel shows 6/7.
  4. A blank envelope: tap two cards you see. Best fit ranks the lit envelopes.
  5. File it, or keep it aside as unknown.
  6. The right page is past usual transit. A red envelope carries its stamp: lost or refunded.

Better than a phone list

Thickness and age cut the field to two or three envelopes. The fold means something: left is in transit, right is past usual transit.

Blank envelopes become a short guess, not a search.

Biggest risk

Thickness is a guess from card count; sellers pack differently. A busy week may crowd one pigeonhole with ten envelopes, and the frame stops narrowing.
