Find the Package

Designer kind: directed. Direction: grouped scanning: no typing; group and filter by seller, state and date so the eye finds it.

The Packages tab, for the moment an envelope is in one hand and the phone in the other. Three concepts, each built on one key you can read off an envelope or remember: who sent it, roughly when you bought it, or both at once with the state. None needs the keys. The Find key stays at the band's end for an order number. Each concept draws the folded Duo, the folded Duo at AX5, the open Duo and the iPhone 18 Pro. Every frame works: tap a seller, a week, a tab or a package, and tick a card in.

Pigeonholes New: seller grid, age band

The return address is the one thing every envelope carries. Packages opens on a grid of sellers, A to Z, one hole each, like the sorting frame at a post office. A hole shows how many packages that seller still owes and a small box for each one: plain for nothing in, manila for some in, amber or red when it is late. Tap the hole and that seller's open packages slide in, as today's cards, ready to tick. The band under the grid narrows the holes by age.

Gain: one tap from envelope to cards for the common case, and a seller always sits in the same place in the alphabet. Loss: an envelope with no legible sender gets no help here; with 120 sellers owing the grid runs to about 40 rows on the folded Duo, so it leans on the age band. The open Duo uses its width: holes on the left page, that seller's cards on the right.

Week Ruler New: slips by order week, week ruler

"Roughly when I bought it" is the second thing a person knows. Packages becomes a list of slips, one line per package, grouped under the week it was ordered, newest first. The band holds a ruler of weeks: each mark is as tall as the number of packages still owed that week, amber from two weeks, red from 30 days, the lost-mail tiers. Tap a week and the list jumps to it; scroll and the ruler follows. Tap a slip and it opens in place into today's card.

Gain: time is visible at a glance, the late weeks stand out by colour, and the band is navigation, not a filter, so nothing on the list is hidden. Loss: it needs two looks, week then seller; a slip shows only the first card names. On the open Duo the slips stay on the left page and the opened card fills the right.

Thumb Index New: thumb tabs, age and state rows, fit count

For the envelope with a smudged label: narrow by everything you half know, at once. The band is a tray of three rows of tabs, cut like the thumb index of a ledger: seller initials in six ranges, the age of the order, and what has come in so far. Every tap shrinks the list above and the count says how many still fit. When it reads 3, you can read them all.

Gain: works from any one clue, and combines all three keys from the direction. Loss: the tray is about 190 pt tall on one page, so the list above is short until the masthead scrolls away; three filters that hide rows are more to undo. On the open Duo the left page becomes a full sorting desk, A to Z keys and all, and the right page holds the matches.
