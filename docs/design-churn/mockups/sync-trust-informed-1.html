Did my phone and my laptop agree?

Designer kind: informed (sync-trust-informed-1). The native app after a sync: the merge notice, the copy that lost, the kept copy, and counts that can be old. Two options: Slip, a port of the web's merge notice, and Postmark, a bold one that puts the last agreement on the letterhead. Every frame is live: tap the tabs, rows and controls. The dashed buttons play what the other device or the disk does.

Today

What main draws now. A merge lands with no word (debt native-merge-notice-unbuilt). The damaged-load line names a kept copy that nothing opens (native-kept-copy-has-no-route). A Tally check-in leaves Packages on old rows until a Packages check-in or a restart (native-masthead-stale-after-tabs). Native has no 24-hour stale line (SyncPanel.row).

Slip

Port. The web's Notice under the letterhead, with its list of changes and a way to the copy that lost.

After a merge that changed something, a green slip under the letterhead says what came in and what was lost, in the bundle's mergeSummary words. Changes lays the changed cards over the list (on the open Duo, on the right page). When the other device's later change won, the panel leads to History, where the copy from before the merge waits with Restore. The kept copy gets a Share control. Packages reads every write. The web's 24-hour stale line comes to native.

Postmark

Bold. The letterhead carries a postmark: when this phone last agreed with the server. It opens a docket of merges.

A postmark beside the seal reads Agreed 2:02 PM. It stays on every working view, so the answer to the question is always one glance away. When a merge changed something, the postmark lands again with a count. A tap opens the Docket: each merge, the cards it changed, who won each clash, and Restore for the copy from before. After 24 hours with no agreement the postmark turns manila. Kept copies sit at the foot of the Docket.
