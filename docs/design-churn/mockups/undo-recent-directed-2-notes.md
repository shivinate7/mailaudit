# Recent day log, directed designer 2: design notes

Mockup: `undo-recent-directed-2.html` beside this file. Open it from disk. Designer kind: directed. Direction: audit trail: a full day log you can scrub and undo in bulk. Decision: `recent-checks-can-be-undone`. Debts: `recent-log-unbuilt`, `recent-undo-under-bar`.

## Sizes, theme and sources

- Duo folded: 466 x 678 pt. The system tab bar starts at y 595 and is 83 pt tall (`duo-fold-and-keyboard`, outer screen, re-measured 2026-11-08). The status bar foot is unmeasured. It is drawn at the 20 pt floor that `duo-hand-tap-shift` uses.
- Duo open: 951 x 669 pt, fold strip at x 455.5 to 495.5 (the brief and `duo-fold-and-keyboard`). The tab bar is the custom pill under the right page: 64 pt tall, 12 pt up from the bottom, 24 pt in from the page edges (`native/Manifest/DuoSpine.swift`, `DuoBarMetrics` and `DuoBar`). The left page has no bar. Its list ends 24 pt above the screen foot. That 24 pt is my choice: the home-indicator inset is unmeasured.
- Large phone: no size is written. `layout.py devices` prints `unknown` for this repo, the brief names none, and no record holds one. The frame is drawn at 402 x 874 pt. That is the size I know for a current Pro phone, not a measured figure. Its 62 pt status area is assumed too. Its bar borrows the Duo folded bar's 83 pt height. The frame's caption says so.
- Biggest accessibility size: AX5, drawn on the Duo folded. Each style is scaled by Apple's AX5 table: caption 2 goes from 11 to 40 pt, caption 1 from 12 to 43, footnote from 13 to 44, and body from 17 to 53. So mono 10 on caption 2 draws at 36, Cochin 16 on body at 50, Cochin 13.5 on footnote at 46, and the mono 11 UNDO on caption 1 at 39. The navigation bar and the tab bar stay at the default size. The tab bar caps its text the way `DuoBar` caps it (`.dynamicTypeSize(...DynamicTypeSize.large)`). The navigation bar's AX5 size was not read, so it is not drawn larger.
- Theme: light only. The native interface is held to light (`design-parchment-ledger`: `UIUserInterfaceStyle`), so the page has no theme switch.
- Tokens: `shared/tokens.json`. Words are set in Cochin and numbers in Menlo (`typography-cochin-and-mono`, the native `.cochin` and `.mono` fonts in `LedgerTab.swift`). The app chrome (status bar, navigation bar, tab bar) uses the system font.
- Apple pages read 2026-11-21 through the `.json` path, and each answered 200: layout (safe areas, layout by size class), typography (the Large (default) and AX5 tables above), accessibility (44 x 44 pt controls, text enlarged to at least 200 percent), buttons (a 44 x 44 pt hit region), tab bars ("a tab bar is for navigation, not for actions", and it floats above content), lists and tables ("people must enter an edit mode before they can select table items").

## What the readout proves

Each frame has a readout under it. It measures each visible UNDO box and the bulk button against the bar. It also says how many boxes moved on the last press: each row's end cell, and every control outside the list (tray, day switch, tape, right page). It goes red when the first live row is in view without its UNDO. Its scroll test reads the lowest UNDO edge with the list at the top, the middle and the bottom. The readout went red on two planted defects: UNDO moved to the end of an AX5 row, and the open Duo's "Undo this" slot dropped (1 box moved). Driven headless in Chrome on 2026-11-21, both concepts on all four frames passed through a row UNDO, a scrub or a tick, Select, Same order, Arm and the bulk undo. On the open Duo's Audit sheet the drive also covered a row tap, "Undo this", a second press on it, and a tap on a changed row. No press moved a box. No UNDO box or bulk button sat under the bar at any scroll, and each first live row in view showed its UNDO. After a batch, the remaining live rows can sit a scroll below, and the readout says so. The bulk button sits 17 pt above the bar on the folded Duo and the phone, and 19 pt above it on the open Duo. This is a drawing in a browser, not the SwiftUI build. The build must take the same readings (`recent-log-unmeasured`).

## Choices

1. **Recent becomes its own screen** (both concepts). A press on the Recent row pushes a full-height screen in the Ledger tab's navigation stack. Its title is "Recent", the same word as the row (`ux-one-plain-name`). Gain: the list ends above a reserved tray, and the tray ends above the bar. So no UNDO cell can sit under the bar at any scroll, and nothing opens in the flow under the finger. This closes `recent-undo-under-bar` when the web takes the same shape. Loss: one more press to reach the log, and History is no longer in view beside it. Pick: own screen.
   Departs from: `recent-checks-can-be-undone`, "Where the entrance sits" (the panel opens below the row in the page flow, as History's does). Not blocking. Owner grant: yes.

2. **The tray is one reserved box.** Every state of each tray cell sits in the same grid cell. So the box keeps its tallest state's height, and an arm, a cancel or a report moves nothing. This is the pattern of `card-the-action-row-is-reserved`, and Cancel takes a slot kept free all along, as `AskRow` keeps it (`LedgerTab.swift`, `AskRow.restoreRow`). Gain: the readout shows 0 boxes moved on every press. Loss: the tray costs 112 to 128 pt on the folded Duo at rest, which is about one and a half rows. Pick: this.

3. **Concept: Day tape** (`#c=tape`, focus `tape-folded`). The tray holds a ruled tape of today, one tick per action. Live actions are ink, and rows in an end state are a line colour. Drag the tape back, or press the arrow keys on it, to the first wrong tap. Each row since then takes the card fill and an accent gutter bar in place, and a dashed rule marks the oldest. The bar under the tape reads "Undo 5 since 14:32". Gain: "I tapped wrong for the last ten minutes" becomes one drag and one press, and the day's shape shows at a glance. Loss: a pick is always "everything since", so a good tap in the middle goes with the bad ones, unless its row is undone alone first. Pick: Day tape, as the spine of the direction.

4. **The tape gives each action an equal slot.** It is not a clock. A clock scale put the 8 actions of the last 25 minutes in 24 pt of a 7-hour tape, so a drag could not pick between them. With equal slots, 12 actions get about 36 pt each on the folded Duo. An hour mark heads the first action of each hour. Gain: the recent minutes, where a mis-tap sits, get the most room. Loss: quiet hours look as busy as full ones, and a day of 200 actions gives about 2 pt a slot (unmeasured; such a day may need a zoom on the last hour). Pick: equal slots.

5. **The scrub moves no row.** A drag lights rows in place and never scrolls the list. A list that scrolled to follow the cut would move UNDO cells while the person's other thumb may rest on them. Gain: the "moves nothing" rule holds during a drag. Loss: a cut that reaches off screen shows only as the fill on the rows in view and as the line above the bar ("5 to undo, 14:32 to 14:51"). Pick: no scroll.

6. **Concept: Audit sheet** (`#c=sheet`, focus `sheet-folded`). The log is a ruled sheet with a who column, and a segmented row switches days (Today, Yesterday, Thu Nov 19). Select enters an edit mode, as the lists page says iOS does: a tick ring takes the device mark's box, so nothing moves. "Every live row" and "Same order" tick many at once. On the open Duo, a tap on a row shows its full entry on the right page: order, seller, count before and after, device, time and state. Gain: any set of rows, from any day, in one batch, and "I opened the wrong package" is one tap on Same order. Loss: more presses than the tape for "the last ten minutes", and the row body becomes a control on the open Duo only. Pick: keep it as the second concept. Each concept answers a different mistake.

7. **Device on each row** (both concepts). Day tape writes it on the top line ("PHONE 14:51"). Audit sheet draws a phone or computer mark in the gutter, with the device name as its label, and spells it out on the open Duo's right page. The brief asks for it. Gain: "was that the Mac or me?" is answered on the row. Loss: an entry needs a device field. Today an entry holds an id, a time and a subject, so the field changes the shape of each `recent` entry. That touches `saved-state-shape`, and both devices must update first, as that record says for each new field.
   Departs from: `recent-checks-can-be-undone`, "What a row shows" ("A row does not say which device wrote it"). Not blocking. Owner grant: yes.

8. **Bulk undo exists.** A single row's UNDO stays one tap with no arm, as ruled. A batch undoes each live row by its own delta, newest first, through the same function as a row press (`undo-removes-only-its-delta`, `undoRecent`). Rows in an end state are skipped, and the report names them: "5 undone at 14:53. Skipped 1: changed since." Gain: the worst mis-tap, a run of wrong ticks, has one remedy. Loss: a batch has no redo (ruled: no redo), so a wrong batch is fixed by hand. Pick: bulk undo.
   Departs from: `recent-checks-can-be-undone`, "Ruled" ("An undo is one tap with no arm"), which ruled single rows only. Not blocking. Owner grant: yes.

9. **Bulk undo arms first** (`#concept-arm`, focus `arm-first-folded-tray` against `run-at-once-folded-tray`). Options: arm first, where the first press turns the bar red and reads "Yes, undo 5 since 14:32" (or "Yes, undo 3 ticked"), with Cancel in the reserved slot, as `AskRow` arms Restore. Or run at once. Gain of arming: a mis-tap on the bar costs nothing. Loss: two presses. Pick: arm first. A press on any row's UNDO while armed disarms the bar, so its count is read again before it can run.

10. **The bulk bar is red, the row UNDO is accent.** The bar uses Restore's colours: red on redSoft at rest, card on red when armed (`AskRow.restoreRow`). Gain: the batch reads as the bigger act. Loss: none found. Pick: this.

11. **Select mode makes row UNDO inert** (Audit sheet). While ticking, each UNDO reads in inkSoft with no underline, and a press does nothing. Gain: a tick press near the right edge never undoes a row. Loss: to undo one row the person leaves select mode. Pick: inert.

12. **The open Duo spreads Recent on two pages.** The left page holds the log. The right page holds the tape and the batch (Day tape) or the entry and the batch (Audit sheet), with the bulk bar above the custom tab bar. Gain: each pose has its own layout, the bar never crosses the fold, and the batch is read beside the rows it takes. Loss: none found.
     "Undo this" on the right page always holds its 132 pt slot. For an entry in an end state it reads the reason ("Undone 14:53", "Changed since") and does nothing, so Select never slides into the place just pressed.
    Departs from: `DuoSpread.spreads(tab:)` in `native/ManifestKit/Sources/ManifestKit/DuoSpread.swift`, which returns false for `.ledger`. Today the Ledger tab draws one page with the system bar across the fold. Recent would need its own spread case. The code calls that line a default, not an owner ruling. Not blocking.

13. **The largest text (AX5).** Rows stack in one column: the label, the name (up to 3 lines), then UNDO as a full-width 64 pt bar, then the device and time, the count, the seller and the order. UNDO follows the name so that the first row's UNDO shows without a scroll (y 237 to 301 on the folded Duo, Day tape). An end state reads on one line in the same 64 pt. The tray keeps only the tape and the button row. The tape's line is dropped, because the button says the same. Audit sheet's day row becomes one menu button, its day heading is dropped, and its filter and tick-many row follows the rows, in its own reserved box. A finished batch reports in short form, "5 undone, 1 skipped", since each row shows its own end state. Gain: every control stays at least 44 pt, and the first UNDO is in view at rest. Loss: one row shows at a time, and UNDO reads above the seller it belongs to. Pick: this.

14. **Empty state** (`#s=empty`): "Nothing to undo from the last 14 days. Each card you tick shows here." Its one control is Open Packages (`ux-unknown-is-not-empty`). An empty day on Audit sheet has its own sentence, with a control back to today. A device filter that empties a day offers "Show all devices".

15. **Kept as ruled.** Rows freeze while the screen is open, an undo adds no row, and the end states keep their words: `UNDONE` with its time, `CHANGED SINCE` and `NOTHING TO UNDO`. The micro-labels are the ruled ones. Rows have one fixed height of 72 pt (`recent-log` mockup, item 4), and an undone name is struck through (item 7 there). The day headings and the 14-day reach are as ruled.

16. **Repeated words.** `UNDO` on every live row, and the day headings, repeat by design. They are one control per row and data, so `ux-label-once` reads them as data.

## Self-review (design-review applied to this mockup)

- Native or web-like: native. It has a navigation stack with a back button, a system tab bar (the custom pill on the open Duo), a segmented control, an edit-mode select with tick rings, and 44 pt targets. The tape is a custom slider. iOS has no stock control for it, so its whole 44 pt strip is the target, and it takes arrow keys.
- `check_ios_mockup.py` faults the page on `system-font` because content fonts start with Cochin. The repo rules Cochin for content (`typography-cochin-and-mono`), the built native views use it, and house `tokens.md` lets brand fonts appear in content. The committed sibling `duo-hand-tap-shift.html` faults the same way. So I read this as a possible false alarm and left Cochin in. The other two checks (safe area, Duo frames folded first) pass.
- House rules met: answer at the press (the report shows in the tray, where the bar was pressed), no dead end (empty states have a control), a disabled bar says why ("Tick rows to undo them", "Nothing live since 14:28").
